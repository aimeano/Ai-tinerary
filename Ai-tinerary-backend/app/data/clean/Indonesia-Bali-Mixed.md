## 1. Badung

Badung is one of the most beautiful and vibrant regencies in Bali, Indonesia. Known as the heart of Bali's tourism industry, Badung offers an incredible variety of attractions for both domestic and international travelers. From cultural heritage sites and sacred temples to world-class beaches, beach clubs, and natural forests, Badung truly has something for everyone.

Whether you are visiting Bali for relaxation, adventure, culture, or nightlife, Badung Regency is the perfect destination. Below is a complete guide to the best things to do in Badung, highlighting the most popular areas and attractions you should not miss.

Badung Regency covers a wide range of diverse regions, stretching from the lively coastal area of Kuta, the trendy beach town of Canggu, and the dramatic cliffs of Uluwatu, to the peaceful inland areas such as Mengwi and Abiansemal, home to the famous Sangeh Monkey Forest. This unique geographical diversity allows visitors to experience modern beach life, cultural heritage, spiritual landmarks, and natural forests all within one regency.

## 1) Explore Mengwi: Culture, History, and Royal Heritage

Mengwi is a culturally rich area in Badung that offers a glimpse into Bali's royal past and traditional lifestyle. This area is perfect for travelers who want to explore authentic Balinese culture away from the busy beach crowds.

One of the main highlights in Mengwi is Taman Ayun Temple, a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

This beautiful water temple is surrounded by lush gardens and tranquil ponds, making it a peaceful place to learn about Balinese Hindu architecture and spiritual traditions.

## Things to do in Mengwi:

● Visit Taman Ayun Temple

● Walk around traditional villages

● Enjoy local Balinese food

● Experience calm countryside scenery

Mengwi is ideal for cultural travelers and photographers looking for a serene atmosphere.

## 2) Discover Abiansemal: Sangeh Monkey Forest Adventure

Abiansemal is another fascinating area in Badung, best known for Sangeh Monkey Forest. This natural forest sanctuary is home to hundreds of long-tailed macaques living freely among towering nutmeg trees.

Sangeh Monkey Forest is a great destination for nature lovers and families. Visitors can walk through the forest paths, observe monkeys in their natural habitat, and enjoy the cool, fresh air. In the center of the forest, you will also find a sacred temple that adds cultural value to the experience.

## Things to do in Abiansemal:

● Visit Sangeh Monkey Forest

● Take nature walks

● Enjoy eco-tourism experiences

● Learn about local myths and traditions

This destination is perfect for travelers who want a mix of nature, wildlife, and culture.

## 3) Experience Canggu: Beach Life and Finns Beach Club

Canggu is one of the trendiest destinations in Badung, popular among surfers, digital nomads, and young travelers from around the world. Known for its relaxed vibe, black sand beaches, and creative lifestyle, Canggu is a must-visit area.

One of the top attractions in Canggu is Finns Beach Club, a world-famous beachfront venue offering infinity pools, ocean views, live DJs, and international cuisine. It's the perfect place to relax during the day and enjoy Bali's vibrant nightlife in the evening.

## Things to do in Canggu:

● Visit Finns Beach Club

● Surf at Batu Bolong or Berawa Beach

● Explore cafes and co-working spaces

● Enjoy sunset views by the beach

Canggu is ideal for travelers looking for modern Bali experiences combined with beach activities.

## 4) Visit Kuta: Iconic Kuta Beach

Kuta is one of the most iconic tourist areas in Badung and Bali as a whole. Kuta Beach is famous for its long stretch of golden sand, consistent waves, and stunning sunsets. Kuta Beach is a great place for beginner surfers, sunbathers, and travelers who enjoy a lively atmosphere. The area is also surrounded by shopping centers, restaurants, and entertainment spots, making it very convenient for visitors.

## Things to do in Kuta:

● Relax at Kuta Beach

● Learn surfing with local instructors

● Watch sunset by the shoreline

● Shop and enjoy nightlife

Kuta remains a favorite destination for first-time visitors to Bali.

## 5) Enjoy Uluwatu: Kecak Dance and Cliffside Views

Uluwatu is a spectacular area located on the southern coast of Badung, famous for its dramatic cliffs and ocean views. One of the top cultural experiences here is watching the Kecak Dance at Uluwatu Temple.

The Kecak Dance is performed during sunset, with the Indian Ocean as a breathtaking backdrop. This traditional Balinese performance tells stories from the Ramayana and is accompanied by a powerful chorus of chanting voices.

## Things to do in Uluwatu:

● Watch Kecak Dance performance

● Visit Uluwatu Temple

● Enjoy cliffside sunset views

## ● Explore nearby beaches

Uluwatu is perfect for travelers who want a combination of culture, spirituality, and natural beauty.

Badung Regency offers an unmatched diversity of attractions, making it one of the best places to visit in Bali. From cultural landmarks in Mengwi and Abiansemal to modern beach clubs in Canggu, iconic beaches in Kuta, and cultural performances in Uluwatu, Badung caters to every type of traveler.

## 2. Gianyar

Unlike many cities in Bali, the government in Gianyar province has strict sanctions on new condominiums and hotels being built. As a result, the area is home to local entrepreneurs and there's a chance that the Balinese life will be preserved for future generations. This makes the area perfect for travelers who want to go off-the-beaten-path.

The Gianyar night market is known to have some of the best food in Bali and is sold from temporary stalls that pop-up each evening. The market also has handmade crafts, souvenirs, and clothing for sale - usually for a negotiable price. The cooking is truly mouthwatering and will expand your culinary knowledge of Indonesian food much beyond the iconic nasi goreng (Indonesian fried rice). Visitors to the night market can stroll, browse, and sample a variety of dishes thanks to the affordable pricing. A large group can take full advantage of the market by purchasing and sharing a wide variety of meals.

From Ubud, the night market can be reached in just 20 minutes via taxi - drivers will often wait for their passengers and provide a return service. The market is one of the main evening events for visitors staying in the Ubud area. The market and the surrounding area can be quite chaotic during peak hours of operation. Scooters line the side of the road near the market and traffic can get quite backed up.

## 1) The Bali Safari and Marine Park

The Bali Safari and Marine Park covers 100 acres (40 ha) and serves as a home for many endangered species. You'll get to take a safari through the grounds and get a good look at some of the park's most famous species - elephants, Malayan tigers, Komodo dragons, and orangutans. You'll get to see an abundance of marine wildlife in the freshwater aquarium before you visit the water park to cool off, so don't forget your swimsuit!

This tour comes with the jungle hopper ticket, which includes stops to get a special introduction to the tigers (or 'Harimaus) and elephants. These educational presentations include information about threats to these animals' survival in the wild, and how the park works to reverse their endangered status. On the safari through the grounds in a 4x4, you'll have the chance to see hippos, zebras, and Himalayan bears.

Besides viewing lots of fascinating wildlife, you'll also get to have a fascinating cultural experience at the Bali Agung show, where you'll see 150 dancers and musicians take to the stage in colorful costumes, to perform a dance style that's completely unique to Indonesia. This performance depicts a famous legend, and the dancers get some help from shadow puppeteers. As you may have noticed, this tour packs in quite a few activities, but rest assured there's also a refreshing break for afternoon tea at the on-site restaurant.

## 4. Klungkung

1) Klungkung Palace (Puri Semarapura)

The original 17th-century royal palace was burned down by the Dutch in 1908, but some buildings once used by the royal family are still standing. Its original name is 'The Abode of the Gods' or Puri Semarapura in Indonesian.

The Semara Pura palace complex was used as a royal court and residence. It is an amazing example of intricate Balinese architecture. Today, the most prominent remaining buildings are the Floating Pavilion and Taman Gili Palace (Kertha Gosa). We highly recommend taking a tour!

Kertha Gosa Court of Justice i s famous for its painted ceiling - it is the last remaining example of traditional Balinese Kamasan style art influenced by wayang puppet theatres, depicting rows of different moral lessons from Hindu epics.

Next to it, you will also find Bale Kambang (Floating pavilion) surrounded by a lily pond, and featuring beautiful carvings and a traditional thatched roof. It was probably used for events and gatherings by the royal household.

Living members of the royal family live in Puri Agung Sempara - a complex built next to the former palace, which you can also visit and see the black and white photos of the family! There is also a small Semarajaya Museum on the premises, which is included in the ticket. It overviews the history of Bali and has some art and curious artefacts.

## 2) Nyoman Gunarsa Museum

Nyoman Gunarsa is a famous local artist who now owns the Museum of Classical and Modern Art, where you can see his works and many other fantastic Balinese paintings.

Through art in this museum, you can get a glimpse of local life, culture, outfits, and religion. The majestic gates and a little garden around the territory add a nice, magical touch to the museum. It is not maintained in the best way possible, but worth a stop! Plus, it is close to Goa Jepang Klungkung.

## 3) Goa Jepang Klungkung

Easy to spot - right where the big elephant sculpture is, this stop is often overlooked by foreign tourists, and few actually know that it's here.

These caves and tunnels were built by the Japanese army during World War II when East Bali was under the control of Japanese forces.

Today, these caves are a bit creepy and inhabited by bats, and there are no active tours inside to learn more about the history of this place. You won't spend more than 30 minutes here.

## 4) Puputan Monument (Monumen Puputan Klungkung)

Easy to spot, this monument is at one of the main crossroads in Klungkung town, right next to the royal palace and Kertha Gosa.

Puputan is the ritual suicide that the royal family and local Balinese elite performed here in 1908 to show Dutch forces that they would rather die than surrender.

The monument looks like a big column and has a memorial at its base with a brief history of the events and some artefacts.

You don't need a guide to explore it, as all the information is provided in Indonesian and English.

However, there is an entrance fee (about 50,000 IDR or 5 AUD) that you pay at the Royal

Palace, and it grants you free access to the monument.

## 5) Bat Cave Temple (Goa Lawah)

Goa Lawah Temple is one of the nine directional temples - the most important temples for Balinese Hindus.

It was built in the 11th century next to an ancient cave filled with more than a thousand wild bats that still inhabit it!

You can visit the temple for a small entrance fee, and if you can around sunset, the bats will be more active and you can see them flying out of the cave.

Note that you can't enter the cave itself or touch the bats. There is also a huge bat statue by the beach in front of the temple.

6) Tirai Air Terjun Tukad Unda Waterfall

Not exactly a waterfall, Tirai Air Terjun Tukad Unda is actually a cascade formed by a dam on Tukad Unda river, and the spot is very close to the town centre.

Locals love to come here to chill, splash, swim, and take photos, and they lovingly call it a waterfall, so the name stuck.

Due to the tropical heat, the best time to enjoy this spot is sunset.

7) Pura Taman Sari Temple

A hidden gem, a Hindu temple of great cultural significance for Balinese kingdoms, and it is still standing intact!

During the Klungkung kingdom, this temple was a storage place for the weapons of the royal family.

It is very beautiful, but unfortunately, tourists aren't allowed inside; you can still take a photo of the gates or do drone footage.

## 6. Karangasem

1. Lempuyang Temple - The 'Gates of Heaven'

Lempuyang Temple is one of Bali's most famous landmarks, especially because of the popular photo spot at the 'Gates of Heaven.' Many people line up here for Instagram pictures. But instead of waiting, you can hike further up the mountain to see one of the island's most

spiritual areas. It's a journey worth taking if you want something deeper than just photos.

2. Tirta Gangga - The Royal Water Palace

Once a royal palace, Tirta Gangga (which means 'Water of the Ganges') is filled with fountains, ponds, tropical gardens, and statues inspired by Hindu mythology.

There are pools where you can take a dip, and just outside the palace, you'll find some of the most beautiful rice field views in Bali.

3. Ujung Water Palace (Taman Soekasada Ujung) - Bali's Floating Palace

Built in 1919, Ujung Water Palace (Taman Soekasada Ujung or Taman Ujung) is bigger and usually quieter than Tirta Gangga. Its bridges, gazebos, and pools make it feel like a fairytale setting.

Some visitors even find it more impressive than Tirta Gangga.

If you're visiting Taman Ujung, don't miss the unique 'Bale Ship' entrance-it's one of the palace's most iconic features.

4. Besakih Temple - The Mother Temple of Bali Located on the slopes of Mount Agung, Besakih Temple is the largest and most important

temple in Bali.

Standing at about 1,000 meters above sea level, the complex has more than 20 temples and has existed for over a thousand years.

Every year, more than 70 ceremonies are held here, each tied to the wellbeing of Bali and its people.

5. Mount Agung - Bali's Tallest Volcano

Mount Agung is Bali's highest and most sacred volcano. Locals see it as a holy place, and sometimes climbing routes are closed during important ceremonies.

The trek is tough, but reaching the summit rewards you with one of the most breathtaking views on the island.

6. Amed - Diving and Black Sand Beaches Love snorkeling? If yes, then Amed Beach in Karangasem is a must-visit.

Amed is a quiet stretch of fishing villages on Bali's northeast coast. The beaches here are

covered in black volcanic sand, and the area is famous for diving and snorkeling.

It's close to Tulamben, home of the USAT Liberty shipwreck. Amed is also more affordable compared to Bali's busy tourist towns, making it great for budget travelers.

7. Virgin Beach - A Hidden White Sand Spot Unlike most beaches in East Bali, Virgin Beach has soft white sand. The waves can be strong,

so swimmers need to be careful, but it's still a peaceful escape.

You'll find small cafes and facilities here, though it's best to skip the untrained beach massages offered by locals.

8. Sidemen Valley - Rice Fields and Village Life

Sidemen Valley is often called the 'old Ubud' before mass tourism. Here, you can walk through rice terraces, meet local weavers, and enjoy views of Mount Agung.

It's a quiet and authentic area, perfect for slow travel. Some workshops even let you try silver jewelry making, adding a unique cultural touch to your visit.
